What kind of case do you have?

I take the cases other firms pass on. Refinery blasts, oil rig injuries, truck collisions, wrongful death: the serious cases where a family’s future is on the line, anywhere in Texas.

  • Refinery and petrochemicalBlasts, burns, and toxic exposure that trace back to safety shortcuts and cost-cutting on the plant floor.
  • Oil rig and offshorePlatform falls and crew-transfer injuries, under maritime law and the Jones Act that protect offshore workers.
  • Chemical plantReleases, explosions, and the chemical burns and respiratory injuries that follow.
  • Commercial truckRegulatory violations, logbooks, and the evidence a carrier would rather lose, preserved before it disappears.
  • Vehicle collisionsInjuries and deaths caused by negligent drivers: high-speed, impaired, commercial-fleet, and rideshare crashes.
  • Aviation accidentsPrivate, charter, and helicopter crashes, and the maintenance and pilot failures behind them.
  • Railroad accidentsCrossing collisions and rail-worker injuries, and the consequences that follow a family for years.
  • Industrial injuriesWorkplace negligence in aging plants and warehouses with inadequate safety protocols, from falls to equipment failures.
  • Product liabilityUnsafe products brought to market without proper testing: design defects, failures to warn, dangerous machinery.
  • Wrongful deathFamilies who lost someone to another’s negligence.
  • Insurance disputesI confront insurers who deny legitimate claims, through the coverage and bad-faith litigation that forces payment.
  • Catastrophic injuriesCatastrophic injuries are defined by more than the pain they cause. They are defined by how they change a life, leaving it altered in some way forever.

Every case has three questions.

Before I take a case, I answer three questions. Together they decide how the case is built.

  1. 01

    What happened.

    Refinery or truck. Rig or chemical plant. Defective product or wrongful death. The industry and the incident decide who is responsible, which experts the case needs, and which laws apply.

  2. 02

    What was lost.

    A traumatic brain injury is not the same case as a spinal cord injury. A burn survivor lives inside a different future than an amputee. What was lost decides what the case is worth.

    See injuries
  3. 03

    Where it happened.

    Houston, Austin, Dallas, and the Gulf Coast industrial corridor. Each venue has its own rules and its own rhythm. Where the case is filed shapes how it is tried.
