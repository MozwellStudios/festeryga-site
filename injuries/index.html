An injury is not a case.

A traumatic brain injury is not the same case as a spinal cord injury. A burn survivor's future is not an amputee's future. What happened to the body shapes everything: the experts the case needs, the damages the law allows, and the recovery a family will actually live through. I treat each injury on its own terms.

What was lost decides what the case is worth.

  1. 01

    I start with the injury.

    Every case begins with what my client is actually living through. The daily reality of the injury decides what I document, which experts I retain, and what damages the law allows.

  2. 02

    Future care, future wages.

    A spinal cord injury can mean a lifetime of adaptive equipment and lost earning capacity. A burn survivor may need reconstructive care for decades. I build the economic record a jury needs to see the real cost.

  3. 03

    What recovery actually looks like.

    Beyond the numbers, an injury reshapes a life. I account for what my client can still do, what they cannot, and what they need, and I am honest about what the law can and cannot restore.

Injuries I handle.

  • Traumatic brain injuries

    From concussion to severe TBI. Changes to memory, thinking, and behavior that can need care for the rest of a life.

  • Spinal cord injuries

    Complete and incomplete injuries, often from vehicle crashes and falls. Adaptive equipment, changes to the home, and lifetime medical costs.

  • Burns

    Thermal, chemical, and electrical burns. Reconstructive care over years, lasting physical limits, and the emotional weight that comes with them.

  • Amputations

    Traumatic and surgical. Prosthetics, phantom pain, and the changes to work and daily life that follow.

  • Pediatric injuries

    Injuries to children, measured over a lifetime: development, growth, and the care a child will still need as an adult.

  • Wrongful death

    Claims for the families left behind, covering both the financial loss and the human one.
