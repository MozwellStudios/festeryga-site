What kind of injury do you have?

A traumatic brain injury is not the same case as a spinal cord injury. A burn survivor’s future is not an amputee’s. I treat each injury on its own terms.

  • Traumatic brain injuriesFrom concussion to severe TBI. Changes to memory, thinking, and behavior that can need care for the rest of a life.
  • Spinal cord injuriesComplete and incomplete injuries, often from vehicle crashes and falls. Adaptive equipment, changes to the home, and lifetime medical costs.
  • BurnsThermal, chemical, and electrical burns. Reconstructive care over years, lasting physical limits, and the emotional weight that comes with them.
  • AmputationsTraumatic and surgical. Prosthetics, phantom pain, and the changes to work and daily life that follow.
  • Pediatric injuriesInjuries to children, measured over a lifetime: development, growth, and the care a child will still need as an adult.
  • Wrongful deathClaims for the families left behind, covering both the financial loss and the human one.

What was lost decides what the case is worth.

What happened to the body shapes everything: the experts the case needs, the damages the law allows, and the recovery a family will live through.

  1. 01

    I start with the injury.

    Every case begins with what my client is actually living through. The daily reality of the injury decides what I document, which experts I retain, and what damages the law allows.

  2. 02

    Future care, future wages.

    A spinal cord injury can mean a lifetime of adaptive equipment and lost earning capacity. A burn survivor may need reconstructive care for decades. I build the record a jury needs to see the real cost.

  3. 03

    What recovery looks like.

    An injury reshapes a life. I account for what my client can still do, what they cannot, and what they need, and I am honest about what the law can and cannot restore.
