No fee unless there is a recovery.

The straight answer, and what to look for in any contingency fee agreement.

You pay nothing upfront.

When I accept your case, I advance the costs of building it, and I am paid an attorney fee only if I recover money for you. If there is no recovery, you owe me no attorney fee.

That is the whole answer. The details still matter, because contingency fee agreements are not the same from firm to firm.

What it means at Festeryga Law.

My fee is a percentage of the recovery: XX%. It is written into the agreement before any work starts, so you know the number on the first day.

Case expenses are separate from the fee. Filing fees, expert witnesses, depositions, and medical records all cost money. I advance those costs as the case moves. The agreement spells out how they are repaid from a recovery, and what happens to them if there is none. I walk you through both before you sign.

“If a lawyer cannot explain their fee agreement in two sentences, hire a different lawyer.”

Edward Festeryga, founding attorney

Not every contingency fee is the same.

Before you sign with any firm, ask these questions and get the answers in writing:

  1. What is the percentage, and does it change if the case goes to trial?
  2. Who pays the case expenses while the case is open?
  3. Are expenses repaid before or after the fee is calculated?
  4. What do I owe if there is no recovery?

Your situation is specific.

The agreement I would offer depends on the type of case and where it is filed. Call and ask. The first conversation is free, and I will tell you exactly what the agreement would say.
